Signs, Zeros, and Zero-Crossings

How to access the signed pieces of a piecewise-polynomial periodic one-dimensional spline and how to take advantage of them.

Terminology

Signum Function

The signum function is the complex-to-complex mapping

\[\begin{split}{\mathrm{sgn}}:{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}},z\mapsto{\mathrm{sgn}}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}{ll}0,&z=0\\{\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{j}}\,\arg z},&z\neq0.\end{array}\right.\end{split}\]

It maps the origin of the complex plane to itself, and it maps every other point to its closest point on the unit circle. Those points \(z\in{\mathbb{C}}\) such that \({\mathrm{sgn}}(z)=1\) are said to be positive, and those points \(z\in{\mathbb{C}}\) such that \({\mathrm{sgn}}(z)=\left(-1\right)\) are said to be negative; the set of the real numbers can then be partitioned as \({\mathbb{R}}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:{\mathrm{sgn}}(z)=-1\}\cup\{0\}\cup\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:{\mathrm{sgn}}(z)=1\}.\)

Zeros

Consider the piecewise polynomial spline \(f:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\) of nonnegative integer degree \(n.\) If an interval \({\mathbb{X}}\) can be found such that it is of maximal diameter and such that \(\forall x\in{\mathbb{X}}:f(x)=0,\) then this interval is said to be a zero of the spline.

Zero-Crossings

Spline of Null Degree

Consider a spline of degree \(n=0.\) If a proper interval \({\mathbb{X}}\) can be found such that \(\forall x\in{\mathbb{X}}:f(x)=0\) and such that \(f(\inf{\mathbb{X}})\,f(\sup{\mathbb{X}})<0,\) then the interval is said to be a zero-crossing of the spline.

If two proper intervals \({\mathbb{A}}\) and \({\mathbb{B}}\) can be found such that they are mutually disjoint, such that \(\sup{\mathbb{A}}=\inf{\mathbb{B}},\) and such that \(\forall a\in{\mathbb{A}},\forall b\in{\mathbb{B}}:f(a)\,f(b)<0,\) then, the degenerate interval \({\mathbb{X}}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}:\sup{\mathbb{A}}=x=\inf{\mathbb{B}}\}\) is said to be a zero-crossing of the spline.

Spline of Positive Degree

Consider a spline of degree \(n>0.\) If three intervals \({\mathbb{A}},\) \({\mathbb{X}},\) and \({\mathbb{B}}\) can be found such that they are mutually disjoint, such that \(\left(\sup{\mathbb{A}}=\inf{\mathbb{X}}\right)\wedge\left(\sup{\mathbb{X}}=\inf{\mathbb{B}}\right),\) such that \(\forall x\in{\mathbb{X}}:{\mathrm{sgn}}(f(x))=0,\) and such that \(\forall a\in{\mathbb{A}},\forall b\in{\mathbb{B}}:f(a)\,f(b)<0,\) then, \({\mathbb{X}}\) is said to be a zero-crossing of the spline.

Descending or Ascending Zero-Crossings

A descending zero-crossing is such that \(\lim_{x\uparrow\inf{\mathbb{X}}}{\mathrm{sgn}}(f(x))>\lim_{x\downarrow\sup{\mathbb{X}}}{\mathrm{sgn}}(f(x)).\)

An ascending zero-crossing is a zero-crossing that is not descending.
